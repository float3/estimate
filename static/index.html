Estimation game

For a room of people

How wide is your range?

You ask the question. Everyone gives the range they are ninety per cent sure the answer falls in, from their own phone. You get the room's bounds — where it agreed, how far apart it got, who went narrow — and, once you type the answer in, how many ranges contained it.

Nothing to install and nobody signs up. The link to the room is the only key there is, so keep the host link: it is the way back in.

How it goes

  1. You open a room and put the join link on the wall. Everyone scans it and gives a name.
  2. You ask a question with a number for an answer. How many lamp posts are there in Munich? When was the first one lit?
  3. Everyone sends a lower and an upper bound. They can change them until you close the question.
  4. You close it, read out the room's numbers, and reveal the answer.

Why bounds and not guesses

A single guess is almost always wrong, and knowing it is wrong tells you nothing. A range is a claim about how much you know: if you are ninety per cent sure, then nine of your ten ranges ought to contain the answer. Too few and you are overconfident, which is the usual case. Ten out of ten and your ranges are wider than they need to be.
